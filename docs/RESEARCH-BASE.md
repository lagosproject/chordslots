# ChordSlots research base

Review of November 2026. References used to study Harmony's curves, design the audits and compare the composition workflow. The [general plan](GENERAL-PLAN.md) collects the decisions and the next work.

## Tension, expectation and closure

| Source | How it was used in ChordSlots |
|---|---|
| Bigand, Parncutt and Lerdahl (1996), *Perception of musical tension in short chord sequences*. [Article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8668513/) | Grounds the distinction between chord dissonance, tonal function and movement between chords. |
| Navarro-Cáceres, Caetano, Bernardes, Sánchez-Barba and Merchán Sánchez-Jara (2020), *A Computational Model of Tonal Tension Profile of Chord Progressions in the Tonal Interval Space*. [Article](https://doi.org/10.3390/e22111291) · [Materials in the audited revision](https://github.com/merismeris/tonal-tension-TIS/tree/a5ca4a211e4ab34dcc9b7ba4f69cb863d801494f) | Main source of the external audit. The first experiment collects continuous profiles and the second choices of global profiles. The audit reconstructed means from ten usable records and compared 11 progressions and 63 positions. |
| Harrison and Pearce (2020), *Simultaneous consonance in music perception and composition*. [Article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31868392/) · Harrison et al. (2024), *Timbral effects on consonance disentangle psychoacoustic mechanisms and suggest perceptual origins for musical scales*. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-45812-z) | References for distinguishing roughness, harmonicity, familiarity and timbre. They help interpret the Tension calculated from MIDI notes when the final instrument may vary. |
| Cheung et al. (2019), *Uncertainty and Surprise Jointly Predict Musical Pleasure and Amygdala, Hippocampus, and Auditory Cortex Activity*. [Article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31708393/) · Eitel et al. (2024), *Perception of Chord Sequences Modeled with Prediction by Partial Matching, Voice-Leading Distance, and Spectral Pitch-Class Similarity*. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1177/20592043241257654) | References for treating expectation, surprise and voice leading as signals that can contribute different information. They help prevent the rarity of a chord from automatically becoming more Tension. |
| Sears, Caplin and McAdams (2014), *Perceiving the Classical Cadence*. [Article](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1525/mp.2014.31.5.397) · Sears et al. (2020), *Expecting the end*. [Article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0305735618803676) | The first provides closure judgments for classical cadences. The second tracks the expectation of closure during the excerpt. They guide the future check of open and resolved endings. |
| Vuvan and Hughes (2019), *Musical style affects the strength of harmonic expectancy*. [Article](https://opus.uleth.ca/items/dd0ab56b-e30b-49e2-a28f-d8b5595594af) · *Expectation adaptation for rare cadences in music* (2023). [Article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37604028/) | The first measures stylistic fit of V–I and ♭VII–I. The second measures adaptation of expectations and confidence in anticipating an ending. They are references for expectation, whereas Sears provides closure judgments. |
| Barratt-Fitzgerald, Maclean and Milne (2025), *The Effect of Preparation on the Perception of Harmonic Dissonance and Tonal Modulation in Western Listeners*. [Record and repository](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/datasets/the-effect-of-preparation-on-the-perception-of-harmonic-dissonanc/) | Reference located for a possible external cross-check at the excerpt level. The record describes ratings of tension, stability, valence and arousal of 25 excerpts by 58 participants. **Examined on 18 November 2026:** the data are on [OSF](https://osf.io/dxq7n/) (one CSV of per-participant responses, one R script and one zip of stimuli). There are 24 distinct stimuli, of two types: prepared or unprepared dissonance, and modulations by common chord, pivot chord, diminished seventh and direct modulation. The stimuli are PDF scores and WAV audio, without MIDI. The tension means range from 3.74 to 5.09 with standard deviations of 1.2 to 1.5 and about 58 raters per stimulus, so almost all differences between stimuli fall within the error. The OSF record declares no license. The CSV includes demographic data of the participants. **Conclusion:** it is not useful for validating the weight of a tritone term, because it does not study functional progressions within a key. It is more relevant for modulations, which ChordSlots does not model. The raw data must not be included in the repository. |

**Project result.** The contextual model did not show a consistent improvement over the static reference with voicing on the comparable stimuli. The temporal alignment and the inferred key of P9 affect the result. P3 was excluded because of a discrepancy between the MIDI and the audio. It remains to check what context contributes when the engine **chooses** chords to follow a curve.

## Brightness, interpretation and moods

| Source | How it was used in ChordSlots |
|---|---|
| Temperley and Tan (2013), *Emotional Connotations of Diatonic Modes*. [Article](https://davidtemperley.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/temperley-tan.pdf) · Tan and Temperley (2017), follow-up study on familiarity and mode. [Article](https://davidtemperley.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/tan-temperley17.pdf) | They justify studying modal color and the line of fifths. The judgments were about perceived happiness in melodies. That Ionian was rated above Lydian limits a strictly increasing reading of that axis. |
| Smit, Dobrowohl, Schaal, Milne and Herff (2020), *Perceived Emotions of Harmonic Cadences*. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1177/2059204320938635) | Mode and mean pitch height influenced the valence of cadences. It motivated controlling chord and register separately in the [Brightness listening test](CONTINUITY-PLAN.md). |
| Eerola, Friberg and Bresin (2013), *Emotional expression in music: contribution, linearity, and additivity of primary musical cues*. [Article](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00487) · *SyMuPe: Affective and Controllable Symbolic Music Performance* (2025). [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03425) | References for understanding expression as the result of several cues and for studying expressive changes over a MIDI performance. The Energy audit measures velocity, density and MIDI output. |
| Eerola and Vuoskoski (2011), *A comparison of the discrete and dimensional models of emotion in music*. [Soundtracks data](https://github.com/tuomaseerola/soundtracks/) · *GlobalMood* (2025). [Project data](https://cms.mus.cam.ac.uk/datasets/globalmood/) | References for the vocabulary and the limits of mood labels. Soundtracks gathers dimensional and discrete ratings. GlobalMood provides descriptors in different languages, including Spanish. |
| *EmoGen* (2023). [Article](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01229) · *EMOPIA* (2021). [Article and described data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01374) · *DEAM*. [Dataset](https://cvml.unige.ch/databases/DEAM/) | EmoGen serves as a precedent for translating labels into musical attributes. EMOPIA offers piano MIDI with per-clip labels. DEAM offers valence and arousal curves on audio. They were considered as references for interpretation and vocabulary, with harmony mixed with other signals in their stimuli. |

**Project result.** The local edit of Brightness raised the harmonic component in 195 of 240 cases versus regenerating with the same target, also with the register center fixed. The actual pitch height of the voicing may vary even in that condition. Energy showed a velocity response with density dependent on the voicing. The usefulness of combining color and register, and the musical differentiation of the moods, remain pending their respective checks.

## Generation and product comparables

- Herremans and Chew, *MorpheuS*, and Guo et al., *A variational autoencoder for music generation controlled by tonal tension*. Precedents for guiding generation with temporal tension contours. [MorpheuS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04832) · [Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06230)
- Noesis documents an editable tension curve. Harmonia describes tension and brightness targets per slot. Scaler 3 offers collections by mood and Hookpad Aria allows requesting suggestions for a selection. Ableton Stacks generates chords with editable rules. These references served to compare workflows. [Noesis](https://dystopianwaves.gumroad.com/l/pnoesisihgaa) · [Harmonia](https://github.com/TGALLOWAY1/Harmonia) · [Scaler 3](https://scalermusic.com/products/scaler-3/) · [Hookpad Aria](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08122) · [Ableton Stacks](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/midi-tools/)
- The VST's transitions incorporate counts derived from **McGill Billboard via ChoCo** and **RS200**. Attribution, versions and licenses are in [DATA_LICENSES.md](../DATA_LICENSES.md). **Chordonomicon** was studied as a possible additional source of progressions by genre and section. **License review of 18 November 2026:** the dataset's Hugging Face record declares `license: cc-by-nc-4.0`, the GitHub repository declares Apache-2.0 only for the code, and its README says the data were created by "scraping various sources of user-generated progressions". With a non-commercial license and a compiled origin of user content, it must not be used for the prior without express permission from the authors and from the source. ChoCo adds nothing new: its license page shows that the useful open subsets are already used (Billboard, CC0) or are classical (Schubert-Winterreise, CC BY 3.0), and JAAH and the Chordify subset are CC BY-NC-SA. No open, annotated set of electronic or hip-hop music was found. An automatic search for other candidates (Lakh MIDI with automatic chords from Chordino, CC BY-SA 4.0 in its Hugging Face derivative) found only data with estimated chords and no genre label, which are not recommended. [ChoCo](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-02410-w) · [RS200](https://rockcorpus.midside.com/) · [Chordonomicon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22046)

### Search for more corpora (18 November 2026)

Three automatic reviews with primary sources and a manual check of the main licenses. Result: **there is no open, annotated, real corpus of electronic or hip-hop music.**

- **Hooktheory / TheoryTab.** Its terms (hooktheory.com/terms) say: "no third party may copy, scrape, bulk-download, text-and-data mine, or redistribute the website including the TheoryTab database or any substantial portion of it (including using it to train, fine-tune, or evaluate models)", unless there is a written data license or API terms. There is no dump. Written permission is needed.
- **Chordonomicon.** No key ("Each song's tonal key was excluded") and no timings; 12 genres from Spotify. The license is still `cc-by-nc-4.0` in the record, and the origin is Ultimate Guitar. The least risky route is to ask the authors for written permission to publish only aggregated counts relative to the tonic.
- **ChoCo.** Its GitHub declares CC BY 4.0 except for the data derived from Chordify, Mozart and JAAH (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). RS200 is obtained separately (rockcorpus) and its attribution is in `DATA_LICENSES.md`.
- **McGill Billboard.** DDMAL publishes it under CC0 ("made them available legally under a CC0 license"), so the direct source can be cited.
- **Lakh MIDI.** CC BY 4.0, but without chords. Derivatives with automatic chords (LMD Chords, CC BY-SA 4.0 in the record according to one review and unlicensed according to another; MidiCaps, CC BY-SA 4.0): not recommended.
- **Synthetic.** WaivOps NRG-CP (CC BY 4.0, 8-bar EDM loops) and the synthetic MIDI corpus of `squamt` (trap, lo-fi; "no copyright restrictions" is not a license). They reflect the generator that created them, not real music, so they are not useful for a prior.
- **ComMU** (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): non-commercial. **HLSD** (derived from TheoryTab): "only for academic purposes". **IDMT-SMT-CHORD-SEQUENCES**: synthetic and with a contradictory license (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 on the Fraunhofer page).
- **AccoMontage2** (MIT in the repository, 5,762 progressions with tonic and mode). It was downloaded and measured: it stores only roots (no qualities), the tonic field takes four values (it looks like a key-group label), 94% has no style label (r&b 22, dark 32) and its origin (the "Niko" pack) returns 404, so its source license cannot be checked. It is not used. It confirms in degrees what POP909 shows in minor: ♭VII 21.5%, ♭VI 24.5%.
- **POP909** (MIT, 909 songs; Wang et al., ISMIR 2020, arXiv 2008.07142). Chords with qualities (maj, min, sus2, sus4, maj7, min7, 7, dim, hdim7, aug), temporal order and key per song (508 major, 401 minor, no other modes). Origin: piano arrangements of Chinese pop songs. Candidate for R6, with the caveat of the rights of the source songs. Measured in the plan, in "R11 cross-check with a second corpus: POP909".
- **Literature.** No study was found that quantifies V/V7 in minor in pop and rock versus classical practice. The closest: de Clercq and Temperley (2011), Nobile (2016) on "VI-based" minor and de Clercq (2021, MTO 27.4). On electronic and hip-hop: Park's thesis (Indiana, 2025) on chord loops of post-millennium pop (unpublished data) and the statistical modeling of Uplifting trance (PMC5222838). No open transition data.

## Cross-check added by the critical review

Sources incorporated on 18 November 2026 to cross-check findings H1–H8 and tasks R1–R9 of the [general plan](GENERAL-PLAN.md#critical-review-of-the-music-engine). The bibliographic data (authors, year, journal and DOI) were checked with searches. The content is summarized from abstracts and records, not from the full text. It is advisable to read the whole article before basing a design decision on it.

| Source | How it is used in ChordSlots |
|---|---|
| Lerdahl and Krumhansl (2007), *Modeling Tonal Tension*, Music Perception 24(4). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2007.24.4.329) | Tension model with prolongational structure, pitch space, surface tension and **attraction**. Contrasts with H1: ChordSlots approximates distance and dissonance, but not attraction or tritone resolution (R2). |
| Farbood (2012), *A Parametric, Temporal Model of Musical Tension*, Music Perception 29(4). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1525/mp.2012.29.4.387) | Estimates with a web study and a lab study how much each parameter contributes to perceived tension. It is the methodological precedent for fitting weights with listener data (H3, R4). |
| de Clercq and Temperley (2011), *A corpus analysis of rock harmony*, Popular Music 30(1). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S026114301000067X) · [Corpus](https://rockcorpus.midside.com/) | Origin of the RS200 corpus. It presents rock as a harmonic practice of its own. It serves to qualify H5 and H6: the prior and the candidate pool reflect a style, not harmony in general. |
| Bigand and Poulin-Charronnat (2006), *Are we "experienced listeners"?*, Cognition 100. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2005.11.007) | Review of musical abilities that do not depend on formal training. It supports recruiting listeners who are not producers for the perception tests. |
| Collins, Laney, Willis and Garthwaite, *Developing and evaluating computational models of musical style*, AI EDAM. [Article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/2D13038AEC3BB894F1345C63F74F6CF4/core-reader) · Amabile (1982), Consensual Assessment Technique, J. Personality and Social Psychology | Collins et al. adapt Amabile's technique so that independent judges rate outputs of style models. It is the design of R7. I did not locate the text of Pearce and Wiggins (2007) on the evaluation of composition models, so I do not cite it. |

The sources already listed above that support this review are Bigand, Parncutt and Lerdahl (1996) for H1, Harrison and Pearce (2020) for H2, Eerola, Friberg and Bresin (2013), Temperley and Tan (2013) and Smit et al. (2020) for H3 and H4, Vuvan and Hughes (2019) for H5, and Eitel et al. (2024) together with Chordonomicon for H6.

**Limitations.** The result of H5 depends on counting V and V7 in the project's own corpus (R5). Findings H7 and H8 have no source because they are engineering decisions.
